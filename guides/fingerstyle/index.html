Guide · Fingerstyle

Fingerpicking, string by string

Learn picking patterns the way a teacher gives them, with string lanes that show every note and a guide guitar picking along.

For you if you can play a few open chords and want to pick them instead of strumming. New to the app? Start with your first ten minutes.

  1. STEP 01

    Read the string lanes

    The lanes are drawn like tab: six lines with the high e on top, and each note sits on the string you pick.

    1. 1The chord now and the chord next.
    2. 2Beat lights count 1 to 4.
    3. 3The pattern and its meter.
    4. 4Fingers: i, m and a are your index, middle and ring fingers.
    5. 5Thumb: p, in its own color, on the bass strings.
    6. 6A dashed ring marks a bass note off the chord shape. On C, your ring finger hops to the low E.
    7. 7The playhead crosses the bar, and each note pops as it’s picked.
    8. 8Past the bar line, the next chord’s first notes, so you know where your thumb goes.
  2. STEP 02

    Choose a style and a pattern

    Open Options. There are 27 picking patterns in four styles, each listed from easy to hard. Start with the thumb alone, then the beginner arpeggios, then Travis picking, pinches and Carter style.

    1. 1Arpeggio, Travis, Pinch or Carter, with a line on what the style does.
    2. 2Each pattern shows a small picture of its lanes.
    3. 3The check mark shows the pattern your drill uses.
  3. STEP 03

    Picking Hand, then Both Hands

    The routine works here too. Fret Hand gives you time for the change. Picking Hand plays the pattern on open strings, so you can get it into your fingers without the chords. Then Both Hands puts it all together.

    When the pattern feels steady, turn on Speed up as I play in Options › Tempo and let the tempo climb to the top speed you set.

  4. STEP 04

    Write your own pattern

    Select a pattern you like, then tap New Pattern at the bottom of the list. You get your own copy to change, on a grid laid out like the strings.

    1. 1Hear It loops your pattern on your drill’s chords.
    2. 2Tap a cell to place a finger on that string. Two notes in one column make a pinch.
    3. 3The thumb’s rows follow the chord: Bass, Alt and Bass 2 land on the right strings for every chord.

Tips

  • Get the thumb steady first. Thumb Alone is the pattern for that, and everything else sits on top of it.
  • Let your fingers rest lightly near their strings: index on G, middle on B, ring on high e.
  • Pick at a tempo where every note rings. Speed comes from playing it clean, again and again.
