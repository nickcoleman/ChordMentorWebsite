Guide · Getting started

Your first few minutes with ChordMentor

Tell it what you can play, set up a drill on two chords, and play along with the guide guitar one hand at a time.

For anyone who just downloaded the app. You’ll need your guitar, tuned, and a spot where you can hear the device.

  1. STEP 01

    Tell it which chords you know

    The first time you open ChordMentor, the welcome asks which chords you can already play. Your answer sets your starting chords and tempo.

    1. 1Pick the row that fits: None yet, Open chords, Open 7ths, Barre chords or Extended chords.
    2. 2Skip starts you with the defaults.

    To change your level later, open Settings › Help › Welcome and Starting Level. On a Mac, it’s also in the Help menu.

  2. STEP 02

    Set up your first drill

    Strumming opens on a change between two chords. That’s your first drill.

    1. 1Save Drill… keeps it by name, with its chords, pattern, tempo and capo.
    2. 2Tap a chord to swap it, or + to add another, in the order the song goes.
    3. 3Full routine runs Fret Hand for 12 bars, then Strum Hand for 8, then Both Hands until you stop. Tap it to loop one stage instead.
    4. 4Play starts the guide guitar after a count-in. The slider beside it sets the tempo.
  3. STEP 03

    One hand at a time, then both

    Every drill runs the same routine, so each hand learns its job before they work together. The title over the chords tells you which stage you’re in.

    1. 1Fret Hand: one strum per bar, so all your attention goes on the change. Get your fingers to the next shape by beat 1.
    2. 2Strum Hand: no chords. Rest your fretting hand on the strings to deaden them and play the pattern.
    3. 3Both Hands: chords and strumming together, the way you’d play the song.
  4. STEP 04

    See what to practice next

    After a session, ChordMentor asks how clean your changes were at your top tempo: Rough, Getting there or Clean. That’s your call, not a score, and it’s what the Change Map shows.

    1. 1This week sums up your sessions, minutes and sprints.
    2. 2Practice next suggests three changes: the one with your slowest tempo so far, one you haven’t played in a while, and a new one near your level.
    3. 3Drill or Sprint starts that change right away.

Tips

  • Start slower than you think you need to. A change that rings clean at a slow tempo is worth more than a rushed one.
  • A few minutes every day beats an hour once a week.
  • If a change keeps tripping you up, stay in Fret Hand and loop it until your fingers find the shape without looking.
