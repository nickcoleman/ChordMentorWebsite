Guide · Strumming

Strum in time with a guide guitar

Follow the beat lights, change chords on the beat, and work up from quarter-note downs to syncopated patterns.

For you if you can hold a few open chords and want your strumming to keep time. New to the app? Start with your first few minutes.

  1. STEP 01

    Read the stage

    Everything you need while you play sits in one place.

    1. 1The chord you’re playing now.
    2. 2The chord that comes next, so your fingers can get ready.
    3. 3Beat lights count 1 to 4 and show where you are in the bar.
    4. 4The pattern: down and up arrows for strums, X for a muted chuck, blank for a rest. The lit stroke is the one playing.
    5. 5The readout shows the tempo, the bar and beat, and how long you’ve played.
    6. 6Play and Stop.
  2. STEP 02

    Pick a pattern

    Open Options and choose from 13 strum patterns, from Quarter Downs up to Anticipate Beat 3, which brings the next chord in just ahead of the beat.

    1. 1Choose the meter: 4/4 for most songs, 3/4 for waltzes, or 6/8.
    2. 2Each pattern shows its strokes, so you can read it before you play it.
    3. 3The check mark shows the pattern your drill uses.

    Want to hear your own chords over the rhythm? In Options › Sound, set Guide guitar to Muted strums.

  3. STEP 03

    Lock in the rhythm, then let the tempo climb

    Strum Hand plays the pattern with no chords: rest your fretting hand across the strings to deaden them and strum along. It’s a quick way to get a new pattern into your hand before you add the changes.

    When a pattern feels steady, turn on Speed up as I play in Options › Tempo. The tempo rises as you play, up to the top speed you set.

  4. STEP 04

    Write your own pattern

    Make a pattern of your own in Options. It’s saved under Your patterns, ready for any drill.

    1. 1Your own patterns are listed with the built-in ones.
    2. 2Write in eighths or sixteenths.
    3. 3Tap a slot to strum it, mute it (X) or leave a rest. Strokes follow your hand: down on the numbers, up in between.

Tips

  • Keep your strumming hand moving down and up all the time, even on a rest. Just miss the strings.
  • Change chords a little early. Lifting your fingers on the last “and” of the bar is normal, and it sounds fine.
  • Stay at a tempo where every change lands on beat 1 before you raise it.
